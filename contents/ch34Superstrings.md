**Superstring theory** is a proposed framework for combining quantum mechanics
and gravity and, potentially, unifying all the interactions. Instead of treating
fundamental particles as points, it describes them as different vibration
patterns of tiny one-dimensional strings. One of the predicted patterns has the
properties expected of a graviton, a hypothetical quantum carrier of gravity.

The theory generally requires additional spatial dimensions beyond the three
we experience. One possibility is that these dimensions are curled up on scales
too small to observe directly. They are additional directions in the theory's
geometry, not a separate dimension for each particle quantum number.

String effects are often associated with distances near the Planck length,
about $$10^{-35}\text{ m}$$, although the characteristic scale depends on the
model. This does not establish a measured smallest possible distance.

Superstring theory has led to important mathematical ideas and possible
connections between particles, black holes, and the early universe. However,
there is no direct experimental evidence for fundamental strings or their
extra dimensions. Its role as a description of nature remains an open question.

### Section Summary

- Superstring theory describes particles as different vibrations of tiny
  strings and includes a possible quantum description of gravity.
- Extra spatial dimensions are part of the proposed framework.
- Its basic physical predictions have not been experimentally established.
